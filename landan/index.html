The house itself

What this means in real life
A 1978 frame house in Westport burns to the ground. The policy pays to rebuild it up to $1,037,000, and if the contractor's number comes in higher, it keeps going up to $1,296,250. Your State Farm policy stops at $967,125. In Westport, that gap is the difference between finishing the rebuild and writing a check for the rest.

Everything inside

Furniture, TVs, kitchen, clothes, tools, the bikes in the garage. Covered at the house and covered while you carry it elsewhere.
  • Your belongings$725,900
  • How they are paidReplacement cost, new for old
  • Identity theft expenses$15,000
What this means in real life
Smoke damage ruins the living room. Your current policy pays on a "limited" replacement cost basis, which means depreciation comes into play and there are conditions on getting the full amount. This one pays what a new couch costs today. On a $725,900 contents limit, that one line is worth more than almost anything else on the page after the house itself.

A ring slips off in the ocean on vacation. No police report needed, no deductible, and because the value is agreed up front, there is no back and forth about what it was worth. One less policy, one less bill, one less renewal date to track. If you add a piece, it is a phone call.

A guest slips on the front steps in January and breaks a wrist. Their medical bills get paid under the $5,000 medical line, no lawyer needed. The personal injury piece is new versus State Farm: it covers claims like defamation, which come up more than people expect.

A rebuild in Westport is a year, sometimes more. This pays for a comparable place to live, the extra commute, the storage unit, the meals out, for as long as it takes. $79,000 more than your current policy gives you for the same situation.

The boiler cracks in February. State Farm says that is wear and tear, not a covered loss. This policy pays to replace it under equipment breakdown, up to $100,000. The inflation guard means the $1,037,000 quietly becomes about $1,078,000 at renewal so the house does not drift under-insured.
Put it to the test

What happens if…

Three things that actually happen to Westport homeowners, and how each policy responds.

An electrical fire starts in the wall overnight and the house is a loss

Everyone is out safely. The contractor's estimate to rebuild a 1978 home to today's Westport code comes in at $1,150,000. Everything inside is gone.
This policy
Rebuild is covered to $1,037,000 and the 25% cushion picks up the rest, so the full $1,150,000 is paid. Belongings are replaced at new cost up to $725,900. Up to $311,100 covers where you live in the meantime.
State Farm today
Stops at $967,125 for the rebuild. The remaining $182,875 is yours. Belongings pay on a limited replacement basis, and living costs stop at $232,110.

A ring comes off in the water and it is not coming back

Beach day, cold water, and it is gone before anyone notices. It was one of the pieces on the schedule.
This policy
Scheduled jewelry pays the agreed value of that piece. No deductible, no depreciation, no appraisal fight after the fact. One call to us and it is handled.
State Farm today
Also covered, but on a separate policy with its own claim process and its own loss settlement rules. Same protection, more moving parts.

The boiler cracks on the coldest week of the year

No heat, no hot water, and the replacement is $14,000 installed. Nothing dramatic happened. It just failed.
This policy
Equipment breakdown pays to replace it, up to $100,000. Your share is the $2,500 deductible.
State Farm today
Not covered. A mechanical failure is not a covered loss on a standard homeowners policy, and the breakdown option was never added. The $14,000 is out of pocket.
