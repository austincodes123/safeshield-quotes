Let's Be Direct About The Premium

Year one looks high. There's a reason.

No surprises and no spin. Here's exactly what's driving the rate, and what to expect going forward.

1

Five-plus years with no prior auto insurance

This is the single biggest factor in the rate. Carriers price heavily on continuous prior coverage, and a gap of five-plus years tells every underwriter the same thing: there's no recent track record to credit.

It is not a reflection of you as a driver. It is a structural surcharge that automatically begins to unwind the moment you go back on the road insured.

2

We deliberately set high liability limits

We could have quoted you the New Jersey minimum and the price would look lower. We didn't, and here's why: you just closed on a condo at 87 Park Ave with real equity behind your name.

At minimum limits, any serious at-fault accident puts your home and your savings directly in the path of a judgment. We chose 250/500 to put a real wall between the road and everything you've built.

3

New Jersey is a high-cost auto state

NJ has the highest average auto premiums in the country. PIP requirements alone (Personal Injury Protection at 250k of medical, plus income loss) carry significant cost, and they are not optional in New Jersey.

Your quote includes $15,000/250 PIP and primary medical coverage. That's not a sales upsell. That's New Jersey law.

4

You're getting every discount on the table

Multi-Policy (with your condo), Anti-Theft, EFT, Good Payer, and Safe Driver are all stacked. Your total credit reflected in this premium is $2,694.

Without those discounts, this same coverage would land north of $7,500/year. The bundle credit alone is doing real work.

New Jersey Only · Critical Choice
Why we elected the "No Limitation" Lawsuit Option for you.

New Jersey is one of a small handful of states that forces every driver to make a single, binding choice when buying auto insurance: do you want the right to sue for pain and suffering after an accident, or not?

This is called the Lawsuit Option, and most people have never heard of it until it's too late to change. We elected the unlimited version for you. Here's the difference:

Option A · Limited Right
"Limitation on Lawsuit"
Cheaper premium. But if someone else hits you and you suffer chronic back pain, soft tissue injury, depression, anxiety, or anything that doesn't meet a strict legal threshold of "permanent injury" — you cannot sue them for it. Period.
Option B · Full Right · Your Selection
"No Limitation on Lawsuit"
Costs more. But you keep your full constitutional right to recover damages for pain, suffering, emotional distress, and loss of enjoyment of life from an at-fault driver — without having to clear a legal hurdle first.

Our position: the "limited" option saves a couple hundred dollars a year and silently strips away one of the most valuable rights you have as a driver. Once you sign it, you're stuck with it for the term. For a couple in your stage of life — two professionals, a new home, decades of earning potential ahead — that's a trade we will never recommend.

If down the line you want to discuss switching to save premium, we can. But it should be a conscious decision, not a default.

What An Umbrella Actually Does

Real situations. Real numbers.

These aren't hypotheticals from a textbook. These are the kinds of claims umbrellas pay every day.

A
At-fault auto accident with serious injury
You rear-end a vehicle on the GWB. The driver suffers a herniated disc and requires surgery. Judgment lands at $850,000. Without umbrella: Travelers pays $500k, you owe $350k. With umbrella: umbrella pays the gap. You owe $0.
B
Guest injured at your condo
A friend slips on a wet floor at your unit, hits their head, and requires long-term care. Settlement reaches $1.2M. Without umbrella: Travelers condo pays $500k, you owe $700k. With umbrella: umbrella covers the difference.
C
Defamation / personal injury
A social media post you make is alleged to harm someone's professional reputation. Even if you win, defense costs alone can exceed $100,000. Umbrella policies include "personal injury" coverage for libel, slander, and wrongful eviction — your underlying policies do not.
D
Rental car accident abroad
Vacation in Europe, you rent a car, you cause an accident. Foreign jurisdictions can issue judgments that follow you home. Umbrella provides worldwide coverage on covered liability claims — most underlying auto policies stop at U.S. and Canada.
