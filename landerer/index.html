Why Chubb Masterpiece Costs More — and What You're Actually Buying

Chubb's Masterpiece policy isn't priced against standard HO-3 forms because it isn't built like one. The headline feature is guaranteed (extended) replacement cost — Chubb will pay whatever it actually costs to rebuild your home to its original quality after a covered loss, even if that number comes in above your $3,000,000 dwelling limit. Most carriers, including Kingstone below, cap payment at the policy limit plus a fixed percentage. Chubb doesn't cap it at all.

  • Extended/guaranteed replacement cost with no dollar cap on rebuilding, including code-upgrade costs
  • Contents covered at $1,500,000 (Deluxe type) — replaced at today's cost, not depreciated value
  • Auto is written on an agreed value basis ($42,750 on the Palisade) — no depreciation fight at claim time
  • $1,000,000 in home liability plus a scalable excess/umbrella layer up to $10,000,000, all from one carrier with one claims team
  • Chubb holds the #1 spot in J.D. Power's Property Claims Satisfaction Study and one of the lowest complaint indexes of any carrier in the country
  • Portfolio discounts applied automatically for bundling home, auto, and excess together

The Kingstone Alternative — Where the 25% Cushion Comes In

Kingstone's Select Homeowners policy isn't guaranteed replacement cost, but it's not thin coverage either. The dwelling is written at $2,471,000, and Kingstone automatically includes a Specified Additional Amount of Insurance for Coverage A — 25% at no extra charge. That means the real number protecting this home is $3,088,750 — the stated limit plus the built-in cushion — without you paying a separate premium for it.

It's a smart middle ground: real cushion against underinsurance, paired with strong optional coverages already built into this quote — equipment breakdown, service line coverage, water backup, identity fraud expense, and inland flood — for a fraction of the Masterpiece price.
