What the cheaper option costs you

The bar you have to clear

Under Limitation on Lawsuit, your medical bills and lost wages are paid either way. But you can only sue the other driver for pain and suffering if your injury clears a legal threshold. Six categories clear it. Everything else does not.

Injuries that let you sue for pain and suffering

Death
Dismemberment
Significant scarringOr significant disfigurement
Displaced fractureA broken bone out of position
Loss of a fetus
Permanent injuryA body part that will not heal to normal function

Injuries that do not clear it

WhiplashNeck and shoulder strain
Herniated disc that healsMonths of pain, full recovery
Concussion that resolves
Torn ligament, repairedKnee or shoulder surgery you recover from
Clean fractureBroken but not displaced
Chronic back painReal, but hard to prove permanent

Where this lands: you could be out of work for four months with a herniated disc, recover, and collect nothing for the pain. Your bills and wages get paid. The suffering does not. That is the trade you make for the lower premium, and you cannot buy the right back after the crash.
