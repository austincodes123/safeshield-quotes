Your coverage, card by card

The limits that matter, with a plain-English "what this actually means" on each.

Professional Liability

If an interpretation is challenged as a mistake or a miss, this pays the defense and any settlement.
  • Each claim$1,000,000
  • All claims in the year$3,000,000
  • Breach of contract$1,000,000
  • Technology errors$1,000,000
  • Regulatory investigation costs$100,000
  • Deductible$5,000
  • Where you're coveredWorldwide
What this means in real life
A monitor report is later said to have missed an arrhythmia. The patient's family brings a claim. The policy hires the lawyers, handles the defense, and pays any settlement up to $1M for that claim, with $3M available across the year. That protects you and the physician who did the read. Your share is the $5,000 deductible. Lloyd's picks up the rest.

General Liability

Business protection that has nothing to do with medicine, for the rare claim and the contracts that require it.
  • Each claim$1,000,000
  • All claims in the year$3,000,000
  • Damage to rented space$500,000
  • Hired and non-owned auto$100,000
  • Deductible$5,000
What this means in real life
You work remotely, so injuries and property damage are unlikely. This is mostly here because companies you contract with will ask for it, and because it's inexpensive. It also covers a rental car on a business trip and claims like a defamation accusation from marketing material.

Put it to the test

What happens if…

Four situations that could actually happen to CardioSleep, and how the program responds.

A monitor read didn't flag a rhythm that later caused a cardiac event

The most realistic claim in interpretation work. The family says the report should have called for a stat consult.
What responds
Professional Liability. Lloyd's assigns defense counsel, manages the claim, and pays any settlement up to $1,000,000.
Your cost
The $5,000 deductible. Everything else, including the legal bills, is on the policy.

A cardiologist you brought on did the read, and the claim names them and CardioSleep

Exactly the situation that stops doctors from moonlighting. Their own carrier says "not our work."
What responds
The same Professional Liability. Physicians interpreting on CardioSleep's behalf are covered under your policy for that work, so the defense covers both of you.
What keeps it clean
They were board certified, licensed in the patient's state, and doing interpretation only. Your vetting process is the policy's too.

A read from next spring turns into a claim in 2028

Interpretation claims are slow. Symptoms surface later, lawyers get involved later still.
What responds
As long as the policy has been kept in force, the read happened after your start date and the claim is reported while active, so it's covered.
If you ever wind down
An extra 12 months of reporting time is available, so closing the entity doesn't leave you exposed to a late claim.

Someone gets into your email and redirects a payment from Virtuox

The most common cyber loss for a small practice, and it has nothing to do with hackers in hoodies. It's one convincing email.
What responds (if cyber is added)
Cyber Protection pays the fraud loss up to $250,000, and covers the forensics and any patient notifications if records were touched.
Without it
This is out of pocket. That's the honest reason we list it as an option worth considering at about $132 a month.
