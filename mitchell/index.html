One More Thing — Flood

Good news: you're in Zone X.

Chubb — like virtually every standard home carrier — does not cover flood damage. We pulled your FEMA flood zone designation to see exactly what that means for your property.

Zone X · Low Risk
Minimal Flood Risk Area
No federal flood insurance requirement

You're outside the Special Flood Hazard Area.

12 Terhune Drive sits in FEMA Zone X, an area of minimal flood risk. Because you're not in a Special Flood Hazard Area, your lender will not require flood insurance, and the federal mandate doesn't apply to you.

That said — flood is the one peril your Chubb policy explicitly excludes. In coastal Connecticut, even Zone X properties can experience flood losses from heavy rain, drainage failure, or extreme storms. The risk is low. It's not zero.

If you'd like to close that gap, we can write an optional private flood policy through Chubb or a specialty market — typically $400-$800 per year for Zone X properties. Entirely your call. We just want you to know it exists and what it costs.
A Conversation About Claims

Insurance is for the moments you can't absorb.

Once you're a Safeshield client, one of the most valuable things we do is help you decide when to file a claim — and when not to. Every claim, even a small one, has a cost beyond the deductible. This is especially true with a carrier like Chubb, which is selective about who they keep on the books long-term.

— Principle · 01
Every claim follows you for five years.
Carriers share claim data through an industry database called CLUE. Every claim — even a small one, even one that got denied — sits there for five years. With Chubb specifically, frequency matters enormously. They prefer clients who don't file small claims, and reward them with relationship stability and pricing.
— Principle · 02
The deductible is a signal, not just a number.
Your $10,000 deductible isn't random. It's the line where insurance is supposed to take over. If a loss is meaningfully above the deductible, that's exactly what your policy is for — and the deductible waiver above $50K means you pay nothing on a major loss. For the in-between losses, we'll do the math with you in the moment.
— Principle · 03
Frequency matters more than size.
Two small claims in three years can hurt you more than one large claim once a decade. This is especially true on a Chubb account, where your relationship with the carrier directly affects your renewal terms. If you have a small loss and you're not sure, call us first.
The Rule of Thumb
"Would I rather have the cash, or have my Chubb relationship stay strong?"
That's the question, every time. If a loss is so large that paying it yourself would genuinely hurt — file. That's what insurance is for. If a loss is small enough that the cash sting is manageable, preserving your Chubb relationship for the next 30 years is usually worth more than the claim payout. We'll walk through it with you in the moment, not in hindsight.
When something happens, call us before you call the carrier. Not to talk you out of filing — to make sure that when you do, it's for the right reason and structured the right way.
