How far the dwelling coverage actually goes

The policy is written at $585,000 to rebuild the house. If construction costs come in higher than that after a total loss, this policy will pay up to 50% beyond the limit rather than stopping at it. On a home built in 1877, that cushion matters more than it would on new construction.

What's covered

Tap any line to see what it pays for. Every limit below is part of the $2,643 premium.

▸Dwelling $585,000
The house itself — structure, attached fixtures, built-ins. This is the number the rebuild is measured against, and it grows 4% a year automatically to keep pace with construction costs.
▸Other structures $58,500
Anything on the lot not attached to the house — detached garage, shed, fencing, driveway gates, retaining walls.
▸Personal property $409,500
Everything you'd take with you if you moved: furniture, clothing, electronics, tools, appliances. Written at replacement cost, so a claim pays what it costs to buy the item new today rather than what the used item was worth.
▸Loss of use $175,500
If a covered loss makes the house unlivable, this pays rent, hotel, and the extra living costs while it's being repaired. Rebuilding a 19th-century frame house takes time, and this is what funds the gap.
▸Personal liability $500,000
Covers you if someone is injured on the property or you're held responsible for damage elsewhere. Pays the defense costs and the judgment, up to the limit.
▸Medical payments to others $5,000
Pays a guest's medical bills after an injury on the property, no fault or lawsuit required. Usually the fastest way to keep a small incident from becoming a liability claim.
▸Equipment breakdown $100,000
Mechanical and electrical failure of home systems — furnace, boiler, water heater, panel, HVAC, well pump. A standard homeowners policy covers a fire or a burst pipe; it does not cover the equipment simply failing. This does.
▸Water backup & sump overflow $10,000
Water coming back up through drains, sewers, or a failed sump pump. Excluded on a base policy, added here on purpose — it's one of the most common basement losses on older housing stock.
▸Ordinance or law 10% · $58,500
The one that earns its keep on an 1877 house. If you repair after a loss, current code applies — wiring, egress, insulation, structural. Insurance normally pays only to restore what was there. This pays the extra cost of bringing the repaired portion up to code.
▸Identity fraud expense $15,000
Costs of recovering from identity theft — filing fees, notarizing, lost wages for time spent restoring your credit, attorney costs.
▸Fungi, wet or dry rot, bacteria $10,000 / $50,000
$10,000 toward mold remediation following a covered water loss, and $50,000 of related liability protection.
▸Loss assessment $1,000
Covers your share if an association levies an assessment after a covered loss to shared property.
