Finding the right replacement cost

Replacement cost is what it takes to rebuild your home from the ground up, not its market value and not the price a bargain quote wants to show. Foremost's estimate came in at $663,000. Other carriers were pushing you up near $900,000, which you rightly felt was overshooting.

The bundle payoff

Your car gets cheaper when the house comes along

This is the part people miss. Moving the home to Travelers doesn't just simplify things, it directly lowers the auto premium.

The Multi-Policy discount on the Tucson

On its own, the Travelers auto runs $1,566/yr. Add the home to Travelers and the full Multi-Policy discount kicks in on the car, bringing it down to $1,359/yr. Same 100/300 limits, same $500 deductibles, same $0 glass, just $207 less a year because the two policies now live together.

One more worth a look

Should we add an umbrella?

This is entirely optional and 100% your choice, but from a pure coverage standpoint it's worth putting on the table.

Optional · your call

A personal umbrella sits on top of everything

Your home and auto both carry liability limits, $300,000 on the house and 100/300 on the car. An umbrella adds a layer of liability, typically $1,000,000 or more, that stacks on top of both. If a serious at-fault accident or a lawsuit ever ran past your underlying limits, the umbrella is what protects your savings, your home equity, and your future income instead of leaving you exposed.

The reason we're raising it now: you've got a newly permitted young driver in the household. Statistically that's the single biggest factor that pushes a family's liability exposure up, and it's exactly the situation an umbrella is built for. Umbrellas are also one of the least expensive coverages dollar for dollar, often a few hundred dollars a year for a million in protection.
