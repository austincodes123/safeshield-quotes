Why we lead with 30 years

20 years vs 30 years, honestly

You mentioned you'd consider a shorter term to save. Here's the real trade so you can decide with your eyes open, and why we'd only shorten as a last resort.

30-year termRecommended

Locks your rate for three full decades, all the way through your kids growing up, the mortgage being paid off, and into your 60s. Yes it costs a bit more each month, but it covers the years that actually matter and the price never changes. This is the coverage that's still there when you need it, not gone right before.

20-year term

Cheaper each month, but the coverage ends at 54 and 57, right when a policy this size gets expensive to replace and your health is less predictable. It's a real option if budget forces it, but understand the trade: you're saving a little now in exchange for the coverage running out during years you may still need it. If you shorten anything, shorten the younger policy, not both.
