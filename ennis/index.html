Why I'm steering you to $2,500

A higher deductible is a shield, not a compromise.

Here's the part most people miss: in today's market, the fastest way to a rate hike isn't a big claim, it's small, frequent claims. Carriers watch claim frequency as closely as claim size. Every time you file, even for something minor, it can follow you at renewal and push your premium up for years, not just once.

Bumping from your old $1,000 up to $2,500 does two things at once. It lowers your premium up front, on top of the savings you're already getting by moving to Travelers, and it quietly keeps you from filing the small stuff that would hurt you more in the long run. You pocket the savings every single year, and you protect your rate at every renewal down the road.

Staying at $1,000 feels "safer," but you'd pay $242 more every year for the privilege of filing a small claim, which is exactly the kind of claim you shouldn't be filing. Insurance is built for the big losses. Set it up to price that way.

The small-claim trap
Say you have $4,000 of water damage. On a $1,000 deductible you'd net $3,000, but that claim can raise your premium and cost you your loss-free discount for the next 3 to 5 years. The "win" quietly turns into a multi-year loss.
When it actually makes sense
A $60,000 fire or a major storm? That's what you file. You absorb the $2,500, the carrier pays the other $57,500, and that's insurance doing exactly what it's for. Big losses in, small ones stay off your record.
Home · The Detail That Matters

About that dwelling number

Your old Progressive policy carried a replacement cost of $806,000. Travelers' estimator came back at $739,000. Before that gives you pause, here's what's actually going on.

Different Estimator, Not Less Coverage

It's a different calculation, not a lower value.

Every carrier runs its own replacement cost estimator, and they don't all use the same construction inputs, labor tables, or regional cost data. Travelers' model simply calculates it differently than Progressive did, and it landed a little lower. That's a difference in how the two companies do the math, not a sign that your home is suddenly worth less to rebuild.

And here's the important part: you don't need to worry about the gap, because we built this policy with Additional Replacement Cost Protection (rider HQ-420) added on. I'm not even sure that was on your Progressive policy. It adds a blanket 25% cushion on top of the dwelling limit, so your real rebuild ceiling sits well above both numbers.
