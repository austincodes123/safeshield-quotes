Straight Talk on the Number

Why insurers rate restoration work the way they do.

I want to be upfront: these numbers are higher than a personal auto policy, and higher than what this same van will likely cost you in year two. That's not a markup and it's not about Josh's driving. It's about what a restoration company's van actually does for a living - and to an underwriter, that job description is one of the riskiest in contracting. Here's the honest breakdown.

The very thing that makes Restoration1 valuable - showing up fast, in the worst weather, at the worst moments - is exactly what an auto insurer has to price for. Your selling point is their risk profile.

The work happens when driving is most dangerous. Restoration calls don't come on sunny Tuesday afternoons. They spike during storms, deep freezes, and flooding - which means this van is heading out precisely when road conditions are at their worst and most vehicles are parked. Carriers see decades of loss data on emergency-response contractors, and the pattern is baked into the rate before your application is even opened.

Every trip runs on a clock. Water damage gets worse by the hour, mold takes hold within a day or two, and a customer standing in six inches of water expects you now. Underwriters know urgency-driven driving - late nights, unfamiliar addresses, time pressure - generates more frequent and more severe accidents than routine commercial routes. A restoration van is rated closer to an emergency responder than a delivery vehicle, because functionally that's what it is.

It's a heavy, loaded vehicle. A 10,000 lb GVW ProMaster carrying extractors, dehumidifiers, and air movers stops slower and hits harder than a passenger car. When a vehicle in this class is involved in an accident, the claims are bigger - and in New York's legal environment, injury claims involving commercial vehicles routinely reach the kind of numbers that make the $1,000,000 limit on this policy the right call, not a luxury.

It's a new venture, and that's the part that ages off. ABC Restoration Partners has no prior commercial auto history, so both carriers rated this explicitly as new-venture business - the most conservative rating there is. The class risk above is permanent; the new-venture surcharge is not. After a clean first year, more markets open up and we re-shop it.

Your record is the bright spot. Josh's clean MVR earns a credit on both quotes, and the National Indemnity program specifically credits drivers over 36. The class sets the base; your record is what keeps it from being higher.

The plan: bind the right coverage now, run a clean year, and I re-market this at renewal when the new-venture surcharge starts to age off. That's the same playbook we're running on the GL side - where, for the record, your risk class is priced the exact same way and for the exact same reasons.
The Decision

Two options, one real question.

Both programs protect other people from you - that's the $1,000,000 liability limit, and it's identical. The question is whether anything protects the van. Option A covers the ProMaster itself for theft, fire, vandalism, and collision. Option B does not: if the van is damaged or stolen, replacing it is out of pocket.

Pays for injuries and property damage you cause to others in an at-fault accident - their medical bills, their vehicle, their property, and your legal defense, subject to policy terms. "Combined Single Limit" means the full $1,000,000 is available as one pool, however the claim splits. Personal Injury Protection (No-Fault)
$50,000 basic · required in NY
$21/mo
New York's mandatory no-fault coverage. If you or a passenger is hurt in the van, PIP is designed to pay medical bills and a portion of lost earnings regardless of who caused the accident, up to $50,000 per person, subject to policy terms. It exists so injured people get treated first and fault gets sorted out later.

Supplementary Uninsured / Underinsured Motorists
$500,000 Combined Single Limit
$3/mo
Protects you when the other driver is at fault but has no insurance or not enough. New York requires minimum uninsured motorist coverage; SUM raises that protection to $500,000 for your own injuries. Given how many minimally insured drivers are on Westchester roads, this is some of the cheapest real protection on the policy.

$1,000 deductible

Repairs or replaces the van after a crash regardless of fault - whether you hit another vehicle, an object, or it rolls, subject to policy terms and minus your $1,000 deductible. This is the larger physical damage line because a restoration van responding to emergencies simply spends more time on the road, in worse conditions, than an average commercial vehicle.

What this coversClose
One thing this policy does not cover
Auto coverage protects the van - not the equipment riding inside it. Dehumidifiers, air movers, extractors, and meters in the back are contents, and they're addressed under your property and inland marine side, not here. If you're building up the equipment inventory, tell me the replacement value and I'll make sure that side keeps pace. Coverage on this policy also applies only to the specifically described vehicle - a second van added later must be reported and scheduled before it's covered.
