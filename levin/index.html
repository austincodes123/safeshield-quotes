Plain-English Endorsement Guide

What each of these actually does.

Endorsements are the add-ons that quietly do the heavy lifting when something goes wrong. Here's what each one on your Travelers policy means, and a real-life moment where it earns its keep.

25% Additional Replacement Cost (HQ-420)

Pays to rebuild your home even if the cost runs past your dwelling limit, up to an extra $557,250.

When it kicks in: A kitchen fire spreads and the home is a total loss. Post-disaster, lumber and contractor prices have spiked and the rebuild comes in at $2.7M against a $2.229M limit. This buffer covers the overage so you're not paying the gap out of pocket.

Water Back-Up & Sump Overflow — $10,000 (HQ-208)

Covers damage when water backs up through a sewer, drain, or sump pump. A standard policy excludes this.

When it kicks in: A heavy storm overwhelms the sump pump and the finished basement floods with a few inches of backed-up water, ruining flooring and drywall. This pays for the cleanup and repairs.

Buried Utility Lines — $10,000 (HQ-856)

Covers the underground service lines you actually own, water, sewer, electrical, when they fail. The utility company only fixes their side; the pipe on your property is yours.

When it kicks in: The buried sewer line between the house and the street collapses. Excavation and replacement runs $8,000, and this covers it instead of you.

Equipment Breakdown — $50,000 (HQ-855)

Covers the sudden mechanical or electrical failure of your home's systems and major appliances, the stuff a normal policy calls "wear and tear" and won't touch.

When it kicks in: A power surge fries the HVAC control board and the compressor on your central air. Replacing the system runs $12,000, and this steps in.

Personal Property Replacement Cost (HQ-290)

Replaces damaged or stolen belongings at today's brand-new price, with no deduction for age or wear.

When it kicks in: A break-in takes a five-year-old TV and laptop. Instead of a depreciated check for what they're "worth" now, you're paid what it costs to buy them new today.

Personal Injury (HQ-082)

Extends your liability beyond physical injury to things like libel, slander, and defamation, situations a standard homeowners policy doesn't cover.

When it kicks in: A heated post in a neighborhood group leads to a defamation claim against you. This covers your legal defense and any judgment.

Ordinance or Law (in Protect Premier)

Pays the extra cost of rebuilding to current building codes, which are often far stricter than when a home was originally built.

When it kicks in: After a major loss, the town requires updated electrical, a new roof deck, and current insulation code. Those upgrades add $40,000 to the rebuild, and this covers the code-driven difference.

Loss Assessment (in Protect Premier)

Covers your share when an association or shared-community group levies an assessment after a covered loss to common property.

When it kicks in: A shared private road or common structure is damaged and the association bills every homeowner a special assessment. This picks up your portion.

Personal Umbrella — $1,000,000

Sits on top of your home and auto liability and adds a full extra million in protection once those underlying limits are exhausted.

When it kicks in: An at-fault accident results in a $900,000 judgment that blows past your auto liability limit. The umbrella absorbs the excess so it doesn't reach your savings or future income.

Every endorsement above is built into the Travelers premiums already shown, not billed separately. Scenarios are illustrative; actual coverage is governed by the policy terms, limits, and exclusions.
