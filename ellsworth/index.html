Your Home, Four Ways

Your dwelling is insured at $1,695,000 with a full replacement-cost build. The only moving pieces are your all-other-perils (AOP) deductible and your hurricane deductible. Raising either one lowers your premium, because you're taking on a little more of the small stuff in exchange for a lower rate. Here's exactly what each combination costs.

Option AOP Deductible Hurricane Deductible Annual Premium Full Pay
Lowest deductibles $5,000 1% (~$16,950) $6,535 $6,535
Low AOP / higher wind Recommended $5,000 2% (~$33,900) $6,339 $6,339
Higher AOP / low wind $10,000 1% (~$16,950) $6,252 $6,252
Highest deductibles $10,000 2% (~$33,900) $6,056 $6,056
Why we recommend the $5,000 AOP / 2% hurricane option: this is the sweet spot. Your AOP deductible is the one you're actually most likely to touch — a burst pipe, a kitchen fire, a break-in, wind damage that isn't a named storm. Keeping that at $5,000 instead of $10,000 means the everyday claims you'll realistically file cost you half as much out of pocket, for only about $283 more a year than the cheapest option. The hurricane deductible, on the other hand, only ever applies in a named-storm event, which is rare in Fairfield — so accepting the 2% there is where you take your savings without meaningfully increasing your day-to-day risk. In short: keep the deductible low where claims are likely (AOP), and take the higher deductible where claims are rare (hurricane). Every other coverage below is identical across all four options.
