Why this matters more now than it did six months ago.

Before homeownership, a major lawsuit usually targeted what someone earned. Now there's a $750,000 asset with your name on it. Plaintiffs and their attorneys see public property records, and once a judgment exceeds your auto or home liability limit, your personal assets, including the home, are exposed to satisfy the difference.

An umbrella policy doesn't replace your auto or home liability. It sits on top of both, kicking in only after those limits are exhausted. One policy, one premium, covering both sides of your life.

Real-world scenarios it picks up: a guest seriously injured on your property, an at-fault auto accident with severe injuries to another driver, a defamation or libel claim from social media, a dog bite at a friend's house, a teenage driver years from now. Any of these can produce a verdict well over $500,000. Without an umbrella, that gap comes out of your pocket and your home equity.

$100 per year buys $1,000,000 of additional protection. That's roughly 27 cents a day to protect everything you just worked years to buy. It's the easiest yes in this entire proposal.

A Word On Deductibles & Claims

Insurance is for the things that change your life. Not the things that ruin your week.

When we set your deductibles at $1,000 (auto) and $2,500 (home), it wasn't to cut premium for the sake of it. It was because the smartest insureds use insurance for the big stuff and self-fund the small stuff. Here's the philosophy worth understanding before you ever pick up the phone to file a claim.

Every claim you file goes on your CLUE report (the insurance industry's claims database) for seven years. Multiple small claims signal risk, and at renewal, carriers reprice or non-renew. One $800 fender bender claim today can cost you thousands in higher premiums over the next seven years. Higher deductibles save you premium up front and discipline the household into protecting its claims record long-term.

File A Claim For This
  • House fire, major water damage, anything structural
  • Theft of high-value items or break-in
  • Liability incident: someone injured on the property
  • Auto accident with injuries or significant damage
  • Total loss or damage well beyond your deductible
  • Anything where the loss could threaten your finances
Pay Out Of Pocket Instead
  • Small fender benders just over the deductible
  • One broken window or a chipped tile
  • A leaking pipe you can fix for $1,500
  • A cracked phone or laptop screen
  • Theft of items below or near deductible value
  • Anything where filing costs you more in the long run

The rule of thumb: If the loss is roughly 2x your deductible or less, pay it yourself. Save the policy for the events that would actually hurt. Always call us first before filing — we'll walk through it with you and recommend the smart move. That's part of the service.

Addressing Your Second Question

Escrow your home insurance, or pay it standalone?

Both work. The premium is the same either way. The difference is how you want to manage cash flow with your lender.

Most Common
Escrow Through Lender
Insurance is paid out of your monthly mortgage payment by the lender.
  • One monthly payment covers principal, interest, taxes, and insurance
  • Lender holds funds and pays Hartford directly when premium comes due
  • Most lenders require this on first-year, especially for first-time buyers
  • Premium itself does not change. No discount or surcharge for escrowing
  • Lender handles renewal payment automatically once set up
If You Prefer Control
Standalone (Direct Billed)
You pay Hartford directly. Lender only collects taxes (or nothing) in escrow.
  • You pay Hartford in full upfront, or in installments via direct billing
  • Paid in Full Discount is available either way and is currently applied to your quote
  • You control the renewal cycle and see every premium change directly
  • Sometimes lowers your monthly mortgage payment if it removes insurance escrow
  • Requires lender approval to waive insurance escrow. Many waive it with 20%+ down
The straight answer: The Hartford premium is identical whether your lender escrows or you pay direct. It's not a quote-level decision, it's a cash-flow and lender-policy decision. We can bind either way and switch the billing method later without re-quoting. Talk to Tom Watters about whether your lender will waive insurance escrow, and we'll match whatever structure works best for you.

A
Dwelling
The cost to rebuild your home if it's damaged or destroyed. Includes a 50% extended replacement cushion if rebuild costs come in higher than expected.

What This Actually Covers
The structure of your home itself: the walls, the roof, the foundation, plumbing, wiring, attached garages, built-in cabinetry, and permanently installed fixtures. Anything that would stay with the house if you sold it.
Real-World Example
A pipe bursts in the upstairs bathroom while you're out for the weekend. By the time you get home, water has gone through the second floor, soaked into the framing, and dropped into the kitchen ceiling. Why The Limit Is What It Is
$648,000 is the rebuild cost, not the purchase price. Your land doesn't burn down. The Hartford uses construction cost data for Norwalk, your square footage (1,703), build year (1929), and frame construction to set this number. The 50% extended replacement buffer means if rebuild costs come in higher (which they often do post-disaster), you have up to $972,000 in protection without paying for it upfront.
