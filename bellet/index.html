Where It Really Matters

Why NBIC Stands Apart

The price difference looks significant on the surface. Here's what that difference actually buys you.

🏛️
Admitted Carrier
NBIC is admitted in New York, which means you're protected by the NY State Guaranty Fund if the carrier ever becomes insolvent. Both Orchid and Beazley are surplus lines — no such safety net exists with either of them.
🌀
Lower Wind Deductible
NBIC's hurricane deductible is 3% vs Beazley's 5%. On a $1.3M home, that's roughly $39K out of pocket with NBIC versus $66K with Beazley in a named storm. That's a $27K swing — far more than the premium difference.
🛋️
5x More Contents Coverage
NBIC covers $531,600 in personal property versus $100–117K on the other two. For a high-value coastal home, the contents inside often rival the structure itself. This isn't a rounding error — it's a completely different level of protection.
🏨
Loss of Use: $265K vs $40K
If your home is uninhabitable after a storm, NBIC pays up to $265,800 for temporary housing and living expenses. Beazley caps it at $40,000. In Water Mill, that covers about six months of comparable rental. Beazley's limit might not even cover two.
🏗️
Higher Dwelling Coverage
NBIC insures the dwelling at $1,329,000 — $154K more than the other two quotes. Coastal construction costs in the Hamptons are among the highest in the country. Being underinsured at rebuild time is a painful and expensive mistake.
↩️
Flat Cancellation Allowed
If plans change and you need to cancel your policy, NBIC gives you a full refund. Both Orchid and Beazley have a 25% minimum earned premium clause — meaning you forfeit over $1,600 the moment coverage starts, regardless of when you cancel.
