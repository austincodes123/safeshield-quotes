What's inside

Your coverage, card by card

The limits that matter, with a plain-English "what this actually means" on the ones worth understanding.

General Liability

The core protection when someone is injured at your salon or you damage property that isn't yours.
  • Each occurrence$1,000,000
  • General aggregate$2,000,000
  • Products & completed operations$2,000,000
  • Personal & advertising injury$1,000,000
  • Damage to premises rented to you$1,000,000
  • Medical payments$10,000
What this means in real life
A client slips on a freshly mopped floor, or a rolling cart clips someone in the waiting area. This is the policy that pays their claim and your legal defense. The damage to premises limit also protects you if a fire or water event you cause damages the space you lease.

Property & Business Personal Property

Coverage for everything you've put into the space at 1215 Post Rd, from stations to product inventory.
  • Business personal property (RC)$50,000
  • STRETCH enhancement blanket$50,000
  • Property deductible$1,000
  • Wind / hail deductible1%
What this means in real life
Replacement-cost basis means a covered loss pays what it costs to replace your chairs, dryers, and stock today, not their depreciated value. The STRETCH blanket quietly adds dozens of smaller coverages (spoilage, signs, computers, accounts receivable and more) under one shared $50,000 limit.

Employment Practices Liability

Protection when a current, former, or would-be employee brings an employment claim against you.
  • Each claim$25,000
  • Aggregate$25,000
  • Wage & hour defense sub-limit$25,000
  • Retroactive date08/10/2026
What this means in real life
Discrimination, harassment, or wrongful-termination claims are among the most common suits a small employer faces, and defense alone is expensive. This defends and pays covered claims. As you add staff, this is the first limit we'll look at raising.

Cyber Virus & Malware

Coverage for the digital side of the salon, your booking system, POS, and the client data you hold.
  • Policy year limit$25,000
  • Ransom & extortion sub-limit$10,000
  • Business income period of restoration12 months
  • Waiting period12 hours
What this means in real life
If malware locks your booking software or a scammer holds your system for ransom, this responds, including lost income while you're down. Any business that stores client names, numbers, and card info has this exposure, even a salon.

Workers' Compensation

The policy that takes care of your team if they're hurt at work, and takes care of you as the employer.
  • Workers' compensationStatutory
  • Employer's liability, each accident$1,000,000
  • Disease, each employee$1,000,000
  • Disease, policy limit$1,000,000
What this means in real life
If a stylist strains their back or slips on the floor, this pays their medical bills and part of their lost wages, no lawsuit needed. The employer's liability layer sits behind it for the rare case where you're sued directly. Premium is based on projected payroll of $400,000 and trues up at audit. Owners/officers are excluded in CT.
Put it to the test

What happens if…

Four situations that could actually happen at Salon Reset, and how the program responds.

A client slips on a wet floor and breaks a wrist

Third-party bodily injury on your premises, the everyday claim for any business the public walks into.
What responds
General Liability pays the medical claim and your legal defense, up to $1,000,000 per occurrence.
Your cost
No deductible on the liability side. You report it, we handle the rest.

A former stylist files a wrongful-termination and harassment claim

Employment claims are common and expensive, and defense costs pile up whether or not the claim has merit.
What responds
Employment Practices Liability defends the suit and pays a covered judgment or settlement, up to $25,000.
Room to grow
As headcount rises we can raise this limit, it's the coverage most worth scaling.

A stylist strains their back and misses three weeks of work

An on-the-job injury. In Connecticut, workers' comp is how this is handled, and it's required once you have employees.
What responds
Workers' Compensation pays medical bills and a portion of lost wages at statutory limits, with no lawsuit.
If you're sued
Employer's liability adds $1,000,000 behind it for the rare direct claim against you as the employer.

Malware locks your booking system and a ransom is demanded

Your calendar, client contacts, and card data all live in software. When it goes down, so does the day's revenue.
What responds
Cyber virus & malware coverage responds up to $25,000, including a $10,000 ransom/extortion sublimit and lost income.
Your cost
12-hour waiting period before business-income coverage kicks in.
