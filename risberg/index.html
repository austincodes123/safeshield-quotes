The house itself

Fire, storm, wind, a tree through the roof, a pipe that bursts. Everything attached to the house, including the garage, deck, and fence.

  • Extra if rebuilding runs over (50%)$452,500

  • Rebuilding to today's building code+10%

What this means in real life
A 1966 frame house in Stamford burns to the ground. NBIC pays to rebuild it up to $905,000, and if the contractor's number comes in higher, the policy keeps going up to $1,357,500. If the town makes you rebuild to a newer code (electrical, egress, setbacks), the extra 10% picks that up. Your deductible stays at $2,500 no matter how big the loss is, even for a hurricane. A lot of coastal Connecticut policies charge you 2% to 5% of the house value in a hurricane. This one does not.

Everything inside

Furniture, TVs, kitchen, clothes, tools, the bikes in the garage. Covered at the house and covered while you carry it elsewhere.

What this means in real life
Smoke damage ruins the living room. Without the replacement cost upgrade, a five-year-old couch pays out at five-year-old-couch money. With it, which is on this policy, you get what a new one costs today. That one line is worth more than almost anything else on the page after the house itself.

If someone gets hurt

Personal liability. Pays for other people's injuries and property damage you are responsible for, and pays the lawyers.

A guest slips on the front steps in January and breaks a wrist. Their medical bills get paid under the $5,000 medical line, no lawyer needed. If it turns into a lawsuit, the policy defends you and pays up to $500,000. The wrongful eviction and personal injury piece matters if you rent: a tenant dispute that turns legal is covered, too.

If you cannot use the house

When a covered loss makes the house unlivable, this pays what it costs you while it gets fixed.
  • Extra living costs, or lost rent$271,500
What this means in real life
This is the line that works both ways. If you are using it as your second home and a fire takes it out, it pays for you to stay somewhere comparable. If you are renting it out, it pays you the rent you were collecting for as long as the rebuild takes. Either way the loss does not come out of your pocket.

The sump pump fails during a nor'easter and the basement takes on a foot of water. Standard homeowners policies say no to that. This one pays up to $10,000. The equipment breakdown piece is a quiet win too: the boiler cracks in February and it pays to replace it. If you end up with tenants, that is exactly the call you do not want at 11pm.

Put it to the test

What happens if…

Four things that actually happen to Connecticut homeowners, and how this policy responds to each.

A pan is left on the stove and half the first floor is gone

Fire department puts it out, but the kitchen, the living room, and everything in them are a loss. The contractor's estimate comes in at $980,000 to rebuild.
What responds
The house is covered to $905,000, and the 50% cushion picks up the rest, so the full $980,000 is paid. Your belongings are replaced at new cost. Either your hotel or your lost rent is paid until it is done.
Your cost
$2,500. That is it.

Nobody has been there in three weeks and a pipe let go in the upstairs bath

Water ran through two floors. Ceilings down, floors buckled, and mold starting in the walls by the time it is found.
What responds
The house coverage pays the water damage and rebuild. The mold line pays up to $10,000 to remediate. If the boiler or water heater caused it by failing, equipment breakdown replaces that, too.
Worth knowing
Carriers expect the heat kept on (or water shut off) when a house sits empty in winter. Do that, and this is a clean claim. Ask us for the two-minute checklist before your first winter.

Your tenant's friend falls on the back deck and needs surgery

Six months in with a tenant. Their friend catches a loose board, goes down hard, and a lawyer sends a letter a month later.
What responds
Personal liability. NBIC assigns a claims adjuster, hires the defense lawyer, and pays the injury claim up to $500,000. You do not pay the lawyer.
Your cost
Nothing out of pocket. Liability claims have no deductible. This is why we ask you to tell us before the first tenant moves in, so the rental piece is switched on.

A named storm comes up the Sound and peels back a third of the roof

Rain gets into the attic and the second floor before anyone can tarp it. Roof, insulation, drywall, and a bedroom's worth of furniture.
What responds
The house coverage pays the roof and the interior. Belongings paid at replacement cost. Living costs or lost rent paid while it is fixed.
Your cost
$2,500. Your hurricane deductible is a flat $2,500, not a percentage of the house. On a $905,000 home a 2% hurricane deductible would have been $18,100. That difference is built into this policy.
Let's talk about the number

Why it costs what it costs

Straight answer. Here is what is behind the premium, including the one thing pushing it up a bit.

The honest part

A 2024 claim is on the record, and it adds a little

Every carrier sees claim history, and a claim from 2024 is still showing. It bumps this rate up slightly, and there is nothing to do about it today. It stays visible for a few more years and then drops off. When it does, we re-shop the house automatically and pass the difference along. You do not have to remember; that is our job.

1. It is a lot of house to rebuild

$905,000 in rebuild cost plus a 50% cushion is the biggest piece of the premium, and it should be. It is the coverage that matters most, so we did not shave it.

2. We already trimmed it

Credits for the roof age, the protective devices in the house, second-home status, and $2,500 deductibles are all applied. This is the lean version of the right policy, not a stripped one.

3. The extras are cheap for what they do

The 50% rebuild cushion is $219 a year. Equipment breakdown is $32. Water backup is $26. Replacement cost on belongings is the one real add at $498, and it is the one we would never let you skip.

4. The hurricane deductible is flat

A flat $2,500 instead of a percentage of the house. On a home this size that is the difference between $2,500 and $18,000 out of pocket after a storm. It is priced into this number.
