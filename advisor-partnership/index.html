A Working Framework
A conversation starter, lets work through this together

How I'm thinking we could work together.

We already know and trust each other, so this is not a pitch. It is my first draft of how a referral relationship could run, laid out so we can talk through it, poke holes in it, and shape it into something that actually fits our practices. Nothing here is fixed. Consider it a starting point for the conversation.

Where I'm coming from

The idea, in one paragraph

You look after your clients' financial picture. I handle the property and casualty side, the home, the autos, the umbrella, the liability exposure that sits underneath everything you build for them. Those two jobs sit right next to each other and almost never overlap, which is what makes a referral relationship natural.

This would be my first partnership like this, so I want to treat it as a test run for both of us. We start small, see what feels right for your clients and your style, keep what works, and drop what does not. I would rather build something you are genuinely comfortable with than hand you a rigid program.

A few principles to agree on first

How I'd suggest we frame it

These are the ground rules I think keep a relationship like this healthy. Tell me which land for you and which you'd change.

01

Your client stays your client

I am there for P&C only. I never pitch financial products, and I hand the relationship back to you. The referral should make you look good, not put anything at risk.

02

Start narrow, then widen

Rather than opening the floodgates, maybe we pick one or two client situations to try first, a home purchase, an upcoming annual review where you ask if they have an upcoming renewal, or someone whose liability limits worry you, and see how it feels.

03

You set the touch

Some advisors want a warm three-way intro, others just want to pass a name. You tell me how hands-on or hands-off you want to be, and I match it.

04

We measure it honestly

Since this is experimental, we agree up front on what "working" looks like, and we check in after a handful of clients to decide whether and how to keep going.

The process, start to finish

Referral to bind: how it would flow

Here's the full path a referred client would take, step by step, so nothing about my side is a mystery. Each stage has an open question in gold where I'd genuinely want your read, because this is where your input shapes it most.

1
You

You spot the need & pass it over

A client mentions a new home, a teen driver, a rental property, upcoming renewal, or a policy they haven't looked at in years. You send it my way however is easiest, a quick email, a text, or a warm intro.

Your call: How would you want to hand a client off, and how much heads-up do you want before I reach out?
2
Me / Safeshield

I reach out promptly

I contact the client within a day, introduce myself as your partner, and set up a short call to understand what they have and what they need. Because they came from you, I treat them that way, they can expect white glove service.

Your call: Do you want to introduce us first via email or phone call, or would you rather I reach out cold once you've told them to expect me?
3
Client

Easy, secure info gathering

Instead of a stack of forms, the client shares their current coverage through a secure link or uploads their declarations pages. Takes them a couple of minutes and lets me shop it accurately.

Client effort: minimal
4
Me / Safeshield

I shop the market

As an independent broker I'm not tied to one company, so I run the client across our carrier network for the best mix of price and protection, and I flag any real gaps, especially thin liability limits or a missing umbrella.

Worth discussing: Umbrella and liability adequacy is probably where our two worlds overlap most. How closely do you want to be looped in on that piece?
5
Me / Safeshield

I present a plain-English proposal

The client gets a clear side-by-side: here's what you have, here's what I'd recommend, here's the cost, here's why. No jargon.

Your call: Do you want a copy of what I recommend so you can stay aligned, or only a heads-up on the outcome?
6
Client

Client decides & we bind

If it makes sense, I bind it, handle the paperwork, coordinate with any lender, and deliver documents digitally the same day. The old policy gets cancelled cleanly, no gap, no double-billing. If my quote isn't actually better, I tell them to keep what they have.

Bound & documented same day
7
Both of us

I close the loop with you

You get a short note on what was placed and what it saved them. Your client is better protected and you get the credit for the intro. Over a few of these, we can see the pattern and decide what to adjust.

Your call: How often and in what form do you actually want these updates? I'd rather fit your inbox than clutter it.
8
Me / Safeshield

Ongoing: annual re-shop & service

I don't disappear after the bind. Every renewal I re-shop the market to keep their rate honest, handle mid-term changes, and advocate at claim time, so your client stays taken care of long term.

Long-term relationship
A rough division of roles

Who does what

My starting suggestion for how the work splits. Adjust as you see fit.

Your part
  • Flag a client who could use a coverage look, on your timing
  • A warm word so my outreach isn't a surprise
  • Tell me how involved you want to be at each step
My part
  • Handle the entire P&C process end to end
  • Keep you looped in exactly as much as you want
  • Watch the liability and umbrella exposure on the assets you manage
  • Send my own clients your way when they need financial guidance
Things I'd love your take on

Open questions for us

This is the experimental part. I don't have fixed answers here, and I'd rather figure them out with you.
  • What kinds of clients feel right to start with? Home buyers, high-net-worth, business owners, or just whoever comes up naturally?
  • How do we keep it compliant on your side? Whatever your firm needs in writing, we structure it that way from day one.
  • What does a "win" look like to you? Client saves money, tighter coverage, fewer of your headaches, all of the above?
  • How do we handle referrals back to you? I'd like this to run both directions if that's useful to you.
  • When do we check in? After the first few clients, let's sit down and decide what to keep and what to change.
What you can count on either way

A few things that won't change

  • I stay in my lane. P&C only. I'm never pitching your clients on anything financial.
  • No pressure selling. If I can't beat what they have, I'll tell them to keep it. Honesty protects your name more than a sale ever would.
  • You always know where things stand. However light or detailed you want the updates, no black box.
  • We go at your pace. This only continues as long as it's genuinely working for you and your clients.
