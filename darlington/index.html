Three Things That Make This Different

Why this plan is not like the others.

— Differentiator · 01
Built $227K above the carrier's own estimate.
The system valued your rebuild at $573,000. We wrote $800,000 with Guaranteed Replacement Cost. If construction costs run higher at a total loss, the carrier still pays. This is the difference between a limit and a guarantee.
— Differentiator · 02
A flood rider built into a standard home policy.
Virtually no other carrier in Connecticut includes flood coverage on a standard HO-3. Most force you into a separate FEMA policy as a second transaction. This is the kind of detail an agent who shops for you finds — and one who quotes from defaults never does.
— Differentiator · 03
An umbrella that's structurally possible.
A $1M umbrella isn't an add-on you tack on at the end. It requires the auto liability beneath it to meet certain minimums. We restructured your auto to 250/500 — at a lower premium than USAA — specifically so the umbrella could be issued at all.

A Conversation About Claims

Insurance is for the moments you can't absorb.

Once you're a client, one of the most valuable things we'll do is help you decide when to file a claim — and when not to. Most people don't realize that every claim, even a small one, has a cost beyond the deductible. This is how we think about it.

— Principle · 01
Every claim follows you for five years.
Carriers share claim data through an industry database called CLUE. Every claim — even a small one, even one that got denied — sits there for five years. When it's time to renew or shop, those claims affect what you'll pay, sometimes at the next carrier, not just this one. A $3,000 claim today can cost more than $3,000 over the next five years in premium impact.
— Principle · 02
The deductible is a signal, not just a number.
Your $2,500 deductible isn't random. It's the line where insurance is supposed to take over. If a loss is meaningfully above the deductible — a fire, a major water event, a serious accident — that's exactly what your policy is for. If it's just barely over, sometimes paying out of pocket is the smarter long-term play. We can help you do that math when the moment comes.
— Principle · 03
Frequency matters more than size.
Two small claims in three years can hurt you more than one large claim once a decade. Carriers price for frequency — they assume someone who files twice will file again. If you have a small loss and you're not sure, call us first. Sometimes the right move is to handle it ourselves. Sometimes it's to file. Either way, you should never make that decision alone.
The Rule of Thumb
"Would I rather have the cash, or have my premium stay flat?"
That's the question, every time. If a loss is so large that paying it yourself would genuinely hurt — file. That's what insurance is for. If a loss is small enough that the cash sting is manageable, your premium not going up for the next five years is usually worth more than the claim payout. We'll walk through it with you in the moment, not in hindsight.
When something happens, call us before you call the carrier. Not to talk you out of filing — to make sure that when you do, it's for the right reason and structured the right way.
